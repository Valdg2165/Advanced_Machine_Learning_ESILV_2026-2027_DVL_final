01 / Start here · Operations & Decisions

An engineer’s field guide to learning

A forecast describes a possible future. A policy changes it. Learn to defend both with evidence you can reproduce.

6 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Tomorrow's demand.
Today's decision.

A forecast estimates what might happen. A policy chooses what to do. This book makes the reasoning between them visible.

Start with a decision someone actually needs to make

Imagine that you are responsible for a small stockroom. Every morning, someone asks how much demand to expect and how much stock to order. These questions sound similar, but they require different evidence. Forecasting estimates future observations from information available now. Reinforcement learning studies actions whose consequences change the situations encountered later. This book follows those two questions through an intentionally small, inspectable operations case.

You do not need to arrive fluent in every equation. You do need to stop when a claim outruns its evidence. We will practise asking: available when, compared with what, repeated how, and useful for which decision? A simple method with a defensible answer can be excellent engineering work.

Two strands, one standard

The forecasting strand moves from a temporal data contract to seasonal baselines, ETS and SARIMA, rolling-origin comparison, and a protected release. The reinforcement-learning strand moves from a finite inventory environment to reward accounting, Bellman reasoning, tabular Q-learning, and controlled policy evaluation. They share an operational theme. They are separate experiments: the course does not establish an integrated forecast-driven controller.

This is the English companion to Badr’s ESILV teaching: three hours of Time Series and three hours of Reinforcement Learning. The full module also includes six lecture hours coordinated with Sana on preprocessing, ensembles, active learning and evolutionary methods. Those topics provide context here; this book does not replace Sana’s material. The practical programme remains exactly four TD and two TDo, each lasting three hours, on Time Series and RL only.

How to use these pages

Read a short explanation, make a prediction before moving a control, then compare what happened with what you expected. All interactive examples are labelled teaching demonstrations. Their values are invented or generated for explanation; they are not the measured results of your assessed experiments. Use the linked lab notebook to produce your own evidence.

Support offers smaller steps; Core offers fewer prompts; Extension offers optional depth. All routes keep the same scientific standard and full-mark ceiling. Changing route after a checkpoint is a sensible learning decision. The book adds no compulsory task or contact session.

Open the Time Series lecture PDF or open the Reinforcement Learning lecture PDF.

Pause and decide

A complicated model loses to a seasonal baseline on the frozen comparison. Has the pair failed the course?

Compare your reasoning

No. A sound comparison and an honest recommendation can meet the full Core standard. Hiding the baseline, changing the split after seeing results, or claiming improvement anyway would invalidate the scientific claim.

Support · Find your starting point

Begin with the diagnostic and identify one recovery need: indexing arrays, reading a probability, or interpreting an average error. Use the guided notebook for that need and explain one checkpoint aloud to your partner.

Extension · Challenge the boundary

Sketch the additional experiment needed to connect forecasts to ordering decisions. Identify the baseline controller, decision metric and information boundary. This is an optional research question, not a new assessed deliverable.

02 / The course’s scientific contract

A claim is a promise to show your working

Make each conclusion small enough to test and precise enough for someone else to reconstruct.

6 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

What does “better” commit you to?

A polished chart cannot tell us which data were used, whether the test influenced model selection, or whether the apparent winner was chosen after many unreported attempts. Those are questions about the experiment. Our evidence rule therefore travels with every substantive performance claim: identify the data or environment version, evaluation protocol, baseline, metric, repeated evidence, executable code and configuration, and one material limitation.

This sounds demanding because it is. It is also practical. When a result changes after an update, a precise claim tells you where to investigate. When it does not generalise, the declared boundary tells you which earlier conclusion can still stand.

A toy claim, repaired

Consider three invented development-origin MAEs for method A: 8, 10 and 12 demand units. The corresponding baseline MAEs are 10, 10 and 13 units. Their unweighted means are 10 and 11 units; A has lower mean error by 1 unit, or about 9.1% of the baseline mean. These arithmetic values illustrate a reporting choice. They are not course benchmark results.

“A is 9.1% better” conceals too much. A defensible sentence would state that A reduced mean origin-level MAE across these three specified development origins, with a tie on one origin and no protected-horizon conclusion yet. It would point to the configuration and fold table. Three dependent origins do not justify a universal superiority claim or a confident population-wide effect estimate.

Keep the chain short and visible

  1. Write the decision and comparison before running it.
  2. Preserve the per-origin or per-episode evidence, including failures.
  3. Record which frozen files produced each figure and metric.
  4. Write the narrowest conclusion the evidence supports.
  5. State what would reverse the recommendation.

Your pair’s contribution and AI-use records belong in that chain. Record what a tool helped produce and how a person checked it. Explanations copied from a model are not a substitute for understanding the result. Both students must be able to explain the evidence at the submitted tag.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework is useful context for documenting intended use and evaluating risk. It is not a certificate earned by completing this course, and simulator results do not establish production safety.

Pause and decide

Your first run crashes at one origin. Removing that row improves the average and makes the chart tidy. What belongs in the portfolio?

Compare your reasoning

Keep the failed origin and its diagnostic. A repaired development run requires a recorded reason and a consistent rerun of the affected comparison. Do not silently score only the cases that favour a method. Protected evaluation cannot be reopened to improve the answer.

Support · Build one evidence sentence

Say: on these cases, under this rule, compared with this baseline, we observed this metric, but this limitation remains. Then point to the exact result row. Start with one sentence before building a larger table.

Extension · Audit a denominator

Compare percentage reduction of aggregate MAE with the average of per-origin percentage reductions. Construct a small example where they differ. Explain which quantity the original claim promised and why changing it after inspection is problematic.

03 / Time Series · Information before algorithms

Stand at the forecast origin

A feature belongs in a forecast only if it could have been known when the decision was made.

7 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

Borrow the planner’s calendar

Suppose it is Sunday evening and you must forecast next Monday’s recorded demand, measured in units per day. Sunday is the forecast origin. Monday is horizon one; the following Sunday is horizon seven. Your information boundary closes on Sunday evening. A feature can appear in a spreadsheet today yet still have been unavailable at that historical origin.

A planned promotion may be available ahead of time if its schedule was actually fixed. Tomorrow’s realised sales are unavailable. A stockout flag observed after trading cannot be treated as a future-known input merely because the historical file contains it. Availability is a property of the decision process, not the column name.

Inspect the index before the curve

The assessed development series contains 1,433 consecutive daily observations; the final 28 target values are protected. Check that dates are unique, ordered and gap-free. Check units and missingness. Distinguish zero sales from a missing record. During a recorded stockout, observed sales can be lower than the demand customers would have expressed with unlimited stock. The course target remains recorded demand; do not rename it unconstrained demand.

In a toy week, recorded sales might be 8, 10, 9, 0, 11, 12 and 7 units. The zero could mean no customers, no stock, or a reporting failure. Those explanations imply different actions. Replacing zero with the surrounding average before asking why it occurred may make the curve smoother while making the problem less truthful.

Decomposition helps you ask better questions

An additive description writes observation as trend plus seasonal component plus remainder. It is a diagnostic view, not proof that the components are independent or causal. A weekly rhythm suggests a seven-day baseline; a lasting level shift suggests that old history may be less representative. A remainder can still contain predictive structure.

The workshop lets you inspect a visible past and future boundary. Predict which features become unavailable when you move the origin backwards. The illustration explains leakage; it does not choose the assessed split for you. In TD1, write the availability contract before building baselines.

Pause and decide

You impute a missing Tuesday value using Wednesday’s realised demand, then evaluate a Tuesday-morning forecast. Is the error estimate operationally meaningful?

Compare your reasoning

No. Wednesday supplies future information relative to the decision. Reconstruct preprocessing using only information available at each origin, and record which missingness assumption you made. A transformation fitted on all dates can leak just as a raw future column can.

Support · Draw one boundary

Write the origin on paper. Put each candidate value on its true availability date. If that date lies after the origin, keep the value out of the forecast input. Repeat with a known holiday and an observed stockout.

Extension · Data can arrive late

Design an availability-time column for a report published two days after the observation date. Explain how a valid rolling backtest changes when records can be revised. No extra dataset or assessed experiment is required.

04 / Time Series · Baselines and honest indexing

Make the simple forecast difficult to beat

Mean, last-value and seasonal-naive forecasts expose what a more complex model actually adds.

7 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

A baseline is a serious competitor

Before fitting a model with many moving parts, ask what could be predicted with one transparent rule. The historical mean assumes that a stable central level is useful. Last-value forecasting carries the most recent observation forward. Seasonal-naive forecasting repeats the most recently observed position in the seasonal cycle. These are three different assumptions, not three versions of doing nothing.

For a toy Monday-to-Sunday week with demand 10, 12, 11, 13, 16, 20 and 14 units per day, the historical mean of this seven-day history is about 13.71 units. Last-value predicts 14 for every future day. Seasonal-naive predicts 10 for next Monday, 12 for Tuesday, and so on. These are invented teaching values, not measured forecasts from the course dataset.

Seven days is a cycle, not permission to read ahead

At origin T, a weekly seasonal-naive forecast uses the latest observed matching weekday. For any positive integer horizon h, its index is T + h − 7 × ceil(h / 7). The ceiling is the smallest integer at least as large as its argument. At h = 1 the source is T − 6; at h = 7 it is T; at h = 8 it returns to T − 6. Every copied observation is at or before T.

The tempting expression y[T + h − 7] fails beyond the first week: h = 8 reads T + 1, a future observation. The interactive example makes that boundary visible. Try predicting the day-eight value before extending the horizon.

Measure error in a unit someone understands

Mean absolute error is the average of absolute forecast errors. If toy actual values are 12 and 18 units and forecasts are 10 and 15, MAE is (2 + 3) / 2 = 2.5 demand units. It says nothing by itself about stockout cost, uncertainty or performance at another horizon. The assessed labs use MAE as the primary metric and sMAPE as a diagnostic; sMAPE also needs a declared convention at zero denominators.

TD1 compares all three rules on the same last 28 days of the public development series. That block is not the separate protected 28-day release. For a formal reference, see FPP3 on simple forecasting methods.

Pause and decide

A model has MAE 4 on one block while seasonal-naive has MAE 5 on a different block. What improvement can you claim?

Compare your reasoning

No fair paired improvement is established. Different periods may differ in difficulty. Compare both methods on the same origins, dates and target definition before attributing the difference to the model.

Support · Follow the weekday

Label seven cards Monday through Sunday. Place the last observed week on those cards. Repeat the same cards for each future week without taking a new observation from the future.

Extension · Find where the baseline fails

Describe a sudden level shift and a changing seasonal pattern that would defeat the repeating-week assumption. Propose one predeclared diagnostic slice while leaving the frozen comparison block intact.

05 / Time Series · ETS, SARIMA and diagnostics

Two useful explanations of a series

Choose a small model family for a reason, then ask whether its assumptions survive the data.

8 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Let the structure suggest a candidate

ETS and SARIMA provide different ways to describe temporal structure. Exponential smoothing updates estimates of level, trend and seasonality as observations arrive. ARIMA models dependence after any chosen differencing; SARIMA adds seasonal dependence. Neither family earns preference from its name. The useful question is which assumptions are plausible and which failures you can detect.

ETS: adapt the components

Consider a toy simple-smoothing update with previous level 20 units, new observation 26 units and smoothing weight 0.25. The revised level is 0.25 × 26 + 0.75 × 20 = 21.5 units. A smaller weight changes the estimate more slowly. This arithmetic illustrates the level component only; it is not the complete seasonal ETS model used in TD2.

A trend component can carry systematic movement forward; a damped trend moderates long-horizon extrapolation. Additive seasonality represents roughly constant seasonal amplitude. Multiplicative structure represents amplitude tied to the level and requires appropriate data conditions. A model that smooths gradual change can still struggle with an abrupt policy shift.

SARIMA: describe dependence after transformation

First differencing studies changes between consecutive observations. Seasonal differencing compares corresponding positions one seasonal period apart. Stationarity here concerns stable mean, variance and lag covariance; it is a modelling condition, not a synonym for a flat-looking chart. Differencing without a reason can add noise.

In SARIMA(p,d,q)(P,D,Q) with period m, lowercase orders describe ordinary autoregression, differencing and moving-average error terms; uppercase orders describe their seasonal counterparts. The course’s bounded Core candidate is SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,0,1) with m = 7. Its coefficients describe statistical dependence, not causal effects of interventions.

Use plots to propose, not to certify

The ACF measures correlation at different lags. The PACF asks what a lag adds after accounting for intermediate lags. A seasonal peak is a useful clue; it is not a model-selection verdict. Residual plots can reveal remaining structure, and a Ljung–Box test can reject a specified joint zero-autocorrelation hypothesis. Failing to reject that hypothesis does not prove independence or predictive adequacy.

In TD2, compare seasonal-naive, additive ETS and the bounded SARIMA candidate at identical origins. Keep convergence warnings and failed fits visible. Consult statsmodels’ time-series documentation when checking the precise API and model assumptions.

Pause and decide

Two models have plausible residual plots, but one has lower fitting error. Is the selection finished?

Compare your reasoning

No. Fitting error reuses the training observations. The candidates still need the shared rolling-origin comparison at the declared forecast horizon. Diagnostics can disqualify a model or limit its interpretation without determining the winner alone.

Support · Translate a model into one sentence

For ETS, say which component may change. For SARIMA, say which dependence is modelled after differencing. If either sentence is unclear, sketch a level shift and weekly pattern before reading more notation.

Extension · Sharpen one assumption

Predeclare one alternative seasonal order or damping choice and its compute budget. Explain the failure pattern that motivates it. Compare under the same development protocol only after valid Core evidence.

06 / Time Series · Rolling origins

Rehearse the decision before the future arrives

Recreate several historical decision dates and keep every candidate inside the same information boundary.

7 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

A backtest is a rehearsal with a locked script

Imagine standing at an earlier date with the future temporarily covered. Fit only on the visible history, forecast the next declared horizon, and compare with the observations that later occurred. Then move the origin forward and repeat. Rolling-origin evaluation makes this rehearsal explicit. It does not remove every uncertainty: nearby origins share history, and one series may represent only a narrow operating context.

A toy example uses 35 days of data and a seven-day forecast horizon. With a training window ending on day 21, forecast days 22–28. At the next origin, train through day 28 and forecast days 29–35. An expanding window keeps earlier history; a fixed-width window discards old observations. Either is a protocol choice that must be made before comparing outcomes.

Three candidates across three origins produce nine fold rows. Preserve all rows and warnings. Forty-two forecast cases per model do not constitute 42 independent replications.

Read the distribution before announcing a winner

For invented origin-level MAEs, model A might achieve 3, 4 and 14 units while model B achieves 6, 7 and 8. Both average 7 units, but A has a more severe failure origin. The next step is to inspect that period, not to invent a universal rule that the smoother method must win. Your decision depends on the declared loss, operational constraints and evidence.

The workshop shows which dates enter training and evaluation as an origin moves. Its small timeline is a teaching model. The exact assessed protocol is in TD2; FPP3’s time-series cross-validation chapter explains the general principle.

Pause and decide

The protected 28-day score disappoints you. Can you choose another SARIMA order and submit a better final chart?

Compare your reasoning

No. That score is release evidence for the frozen decision. Record the disappointing result and revise the recommendation. A newly tuned model would require a new, independently protected evaluation before supporting a new final claim.

Support · Audit one row

Write the final training date, first forecast date and final forecast date on one line. Verify that training ends before forecasting starts and that every candidate shares those dates.

Extension · Dependence matters

Explain why overlapping forecast horizons complicate uncertainty estimates for aggregate error. Sketch a more separated origin design and identify its trade-off in the number and representativeness of cases.

07 / Time Series · Intervals and operational limits

A forecast needs room to be wrong

Judge prediction intervals by what they cover, how wide they are, and whether their assumptions survive the task.

7 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

One number hides several possible futures

A point forecast of 30 units does not distinguish a stable process from one that often produces 10 or 50. A prediction interval describes uncertainty about a future observation under a specified method and assumptions. It is different from a confidence interval for an estimated mean. Operationally, the distinction matters: planning stock for the uncertainty of an average can understate the variability of tomorrow’s demand.

For a toy calculation only, suppose future observations are normally distributed with known mean 30 units and standard deviation 5 units. A central 95% interval is approximately 30 ± 1.96 × 5, or 20.2–39.8 units; its width is 19.6 units. Real demand need not be normal, parameters are estimated, and abrupt changes can invalidate the assumptions. This calculation does not establish the calibration of the course’s SARIMA intervals.

Coverage and width belong together

Empirical coverage is the fraction of evaluated observations inside their corresponding intervals. If 17 of 20 toy observations fall inside nominal 95% intervals, observed coverage is 85%. That small sample raises a question; it does not precisely determine long-run coverage. An interval spanning every plausible value can cover well while being too broad to guide a decision.

Inspect coverage and width together, by horizon when evidence permits. Keep lower and upper bounds aligned with dates. Nested 80% and 95% intervals should remain nested. Clipping negative forecasts or bounds changes the forecasting procedure and may affect coverage; document the transformation rather than treating it as cosmetic formatting.

What the course permits you to conclude

In TDo1, reuse TD2’s three 14-day origins and existing point-metric evidence. Report 80% and 95% interval coverage only for methods that actually provide those intervals. Three origins and 42 dependent cases are limited development evidence.

The frozen release rule prefers SARIMA for interval-aware release only when its mean development MAE is within 5% of the best candidate. Otherwise document the fallback and its uncertainty limitation. Generate the separate 28-day release once after freezing that rule; do not reopen model selection. See FPP3 on distributional forecasts and prediction intervals for the underlying interpretation.

Pause and decide

Method A has narrow intervals and 60% observed coverage; method B has wider intervals and 93% coverage, both labelled 95%. Can width alone select A?

Compare your reasoning

No. Narrowness is useful only alongside credible coverage and an appropriate decision context. Inspect the sample size, horizons and failures before choosing either method. Neither observed percentage alone establishes production calibration.

Support · Count before interpreting

For each toy case, check whether lower bound ≤ observation ≤ upper bound. Count the successful cases and divide by the total. Then calculate each interval’s width. These are two different questions.

Extension · Challenge the interval method

Describe how heavy tails, a level shift or stockout censoring could undermine a normal interval. Propose one development-only diagnostic while keeping the point forecasts and selection protocol frozen.

08 / Reinforcement Learning · State, action and reward

Build a world small enough to inspect

Before asking an agent to learn, make the environment’s timing, state and cost accounting explicit.

8 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

The action changes what happens next

Forecasting estimates observations; a policy selects actions. In inventory control, ordering today affects immediate expense, available stock, unmet demand and tomorrow’s state. A Markov decision process makes the experiment’s contract explicit: states, valid actions, transition and reward rules, and the discount factor used to value future consequences.

The course environment is deliberately finite. Stock ranges from 0 to 8 units, the observed demand regime is low or high, and the action indexes an order of 0, 1 or 2 units. The state is the pair (stock_level, demand_regime), giving 18 states. The regime persists probabilistically. Stock alone therefore omits information relevant to the next demand distribution.

Audit the order of events

Replenishment arrives immediately and available stock is capped at 8. Demand is sampled conditional on the current regime. Available units serve demand; remaining units are held and shortages are counted. The next regime is then sampled. This timing is part of the environment version. Delaying replenishment by a day would define a different problem.

One-step cost is 0.45 cost units per ordered unit, plus 0.30 per unit held after demand, plus 3.00 per unit of unmet demand. Reward is negative cost. These coefficients are teaching assumptions, not a claim about a real company’s prices.

Work one transition by hand

In a toy transition consistent with that contract, stock is 1 unit and the action orders 2. Available stock becomes 3. If demand is 3, all three units are served, no stock remains, and no demand is unmet. Cost is 0.90 and reward is −0.90. If the same demand had followed an order of zero, only one unit would be served: two units would be unmet, costing 6.00. This compares immediate outcomes only; it does not prove the optimal long-run action.

Near capacity, the environment charges the ordered quantity even when the cap prevents all of it from increasing available stock. Inspect that assumption. Do not silently repair the simulator to match an imagined warehouse. The workshop exposes one transition; TD3 requires you to test the full public contract and compare transparent baselines.

Pause and decide

Two states have the same stock but different observed demand regimes. Should their actions necessarily be identical?

Compare your reasoning

No. The regimes imply different transition and demand probabilities. A policy may choose the same action in both states, but the representation must permit a justified difference. Omitting the regime changes the intended Markov state.

Support · Follow units through the shelf

Track beginning stock, ordered units, available units, served demand, remaining stock and unmet demand separately. Reconstruct each cost term before discussing learning.

Extension · Find the missing state

Suppose orders arrive after two days. Explain why current stock and regime are no longer sufficient and identify the smallest pipeline information a state would need. This is an optional alternative model, not a change to the assessed environment.

09 / Reinforcement Learning · Return and value

Let tomorrow enter today’s calculation

Bellman reasoning links an immediate reward to the value of what can happen next.

7 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

A cheap action today can be expensive over time

A policy tells us which action to take. A value estimates the discounted return obtained by following a specified continuation. For rewards R1, R2, R3 and discount factor γ, a three-step illustrative return is R1 + γR2 + γ²R3. Discounting makes the time preference explicit; it does not turn a poor reward definition into a good objective.

Take invented rewards −2, −1 and −3 cost units and γ = 0.9. The three-step return is −2 − 0.9 − 2.43 = −5.33 cost units. All else equal, a less negative return is preferable. This short calculation is a teaching example, not a measured policy result or the full continuing-task return.

The interactive widget previews the sampled Q-learning update developed in the next chapter. It adds a learning rate to the immediate-reward-plus-continuation target. Read the expectation example here first, then use the widget to compare true termination with truncation.

Value is recursive

Bellman expectation asks for immediate reward plus discounted next-state value, averaged over the actions a given policy takes and the transitions the environment produces. Bellman optimality changes the continuation to the best available next action. The expectation over possible transitions and the maximum over possible actions perform different jobs.

For a separate toy action, suppose immediate reward is −2. Its next state is A with probability 0.75 and B with probability 0.25. Suppose the proposed continuation values are −4 and −8 cost units. Expected continuation is −5, so with γ = 0.9 this action’s one-step backup is −2 + 0.9 × (−5) = −6.5. It is a backup using supplied estimates; it is not proof that those estimates are correct or optimal.

Do not confuse the recorder stopping with the world ending

The course environment models a continuing task through 40-step recorded rollouts. It reports truncation at the time limit, not true termination. A learning update therefore retains the continuation estimate at that boundary. A genuinely terminal transition has no future return and removes that continuation. The rollout loop still stops when either flag is raised; stopping data collection and zeroing future value are different decisions.

The Gymnasium time-limit guide explains this distinction. For the foundations of return and Bellman equations, use Sutton and Barto’s author-hosted textbook page. If that host is unavailable, use the verified MIT Press publisher record and access links; the publisher record does not by itself guarantee full-text access.

Pause and decide

Your notebook reaches step 40 with a healthy stockroom. Should its continuation value become zero because the recording ends?

Compare your reasoning

No. In this continuing environment, step 40 is truncation. Keep the continuation term for learning while ending the recorded rollout. Zero continuation is appropriate for true termination, which the course environment does not produce.

Support · Separate three quantities

Write immediate reward, expected next value and discount factor on separate lines. Combine them only after checking the units and signs. Negative reward often means cost, not a broken implementation.

Extension · When time really is state

Imagine a finite contract that genuinely ends after 40 steps. Explain why remaining time may need to enter the state and why the optimal action near its end can differ from the continuing-task policy.

10 / Reinforcement Learning · Exploration and Q-learning

Learn from experience without mistaking luck for skill

A sampled transition can update a value estimate. It cannot, by itself, establish that a policy is good.

8 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

Experience is selective when your policy creates it

An agent that always chooses the action currently ranked first may never discover a better alternative. Exploration creates observations of actions that present estimates undervalue. Exploitation uses the best present estimate. Neither strategy guarantees good evidence by itself: random wandering can waste effort, while an early lucky observation can dominate a purely greedy agent.

Under the usual epsilon-greedy convention, the agent chooses a uniformly random valid action with probability ε and otherwise chooses a greedy action. With three actions, one uniquely greedy action and ε = 0.30, that greedy action is selected with probability 0.70 + 0.30 / 3 = 0.80. Each other action has probability 0.10. Exploration can select the greedy action too. This is probability arithmetic, not a result from a trained course policy.

A Q update moves one estimate toward a target

Q-learning combines observed reward with a greedy next-state estimate. For a non-terminal transition, the target is reward plus γ times the largest next-state Q value. The difference between target and current Q is the temporal-difference error. The learning rate α controls what fraction of that error changes the current estimate.

Use a toy transition with current Q = −5, reward = −2, γ = 0.90, best next Q = −4 and α = 0.20. Target is −5.6; error is −0.6; updated Q is −5.12. You should be able to audit this arithmetic before implementing the assessed loop. The book gives conceptual examples, not a completed training implementation.

Record what learning actually did

The table has shape (9, 2, 3): stock, regime and action. It must remain finite. Training configuration includes the learning rate, discount, exploration schedule, budget and declared random seeds. A decaying exploration schedule is part of the method, not a detail to omit from the report.

In TD4, implement and audit the loop, then freeze the resulting policy before independent development evaluation. Preserve the distinction between true termination and time-limit truncation. A favourable training curve is neither a convergence proof nor final evidence of policy quality. Classical convergence arguments require assumptions about visitation, step sizes, finite states and stationary dynamics.

Pause and decide

Two runs use the same budget but different early trajectories. One learns quickly and the other does not. Can you report only the first as the algorithm’s performance?

Compare your reasoning

No. The variation is part of the result. Report the declared repeated runs, their dispersion and the selection rule. Choosing the favourable seed after inspection turns an experimental factor into an unreported tuning decision.

Support · Audit one update

Calculate target, error and new Q in separate steps. Check that α = 0 leaves Q unchanged and α = 1 replaces it with the target. Then explain which boundary condition changes the target.

Extension · Investigate a mechanism

After valid Core work, predeclare one learning-rate or exploration sensitivity question under a fixed compute budget. Explain which mechanism it tests. A larger sweep without a question is not automatically deeper work.

11 / Reinforcement Learning · Frozen policies and paired evidence

Give every policy the same difficult day

Compare cost, service and failure cases on shared evaluation scenarios after learning has stopped.

8 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Enable JavaScript for the experiment. The chapter below includes a worked example.

Evaluate what you would actually run

Once learning finishes, freeze the Q-table and policy rule. Disable updates. Disable exploratory actions unless exploration is explicitly part of the intended deployed policy. Run evaluation scenarios separate from training. Compare against both random valid actions and the transparent reorder heuristic using the same environment version, cost coefficients and evaluation scenarios.

Common scenarios make differences easier to interpret, but matching seed labels alone is not a universal fairness guarantee. It works as intended only when environment random draws are aligned across policies. The course environment’s demand and regime sequence is exogenous to the action under its declared implementation; preserve that contract rather than changing how random numbers are consumed.

Use paired differences, then inspect what they conceal

In a toy three-scenario comparison, heuristic costs are 20, 30 and 40 cost units per rollout. Learned-policy costs are 18, 35 and 32. Learned minus heuristic differences are −2, +5 and −8, averaging about −1.67 cost units. The learned policy costs less on average in these invented scenarios but more in the second. Three cases do not establish a reliable population-level advantage.

A mean cost also hides service failures. Report unmet units, mean stock and order quantities alongside return. The course evaluator’s fill-rate measure is the mean of per-step served-demand fractions, with zero-demand steps assigned 1. It is not the same as total served units divided by total demand over a rollout.

For a toy two-step example, demand is 0 then 2 units, with 0 then 1 served. Mean step fill rate is (1 + 0.5) / 2 = 0.75, whereas aggregate demand-weighted fill rate is 1 / 2 = 0.50. Neither can be substituted silently for the other. A KPI’s definition is part of the claim.

Keep the release boundary intact

TDo2 submits frozen artifacts to a seed-injected controlled evaluator. Protected seeds and individual controlled episode rows remain instructor-only. Students receive aggregate evidence and a receipt tied to their artifacts. The course reports Student-t intervals for mean cost and fill rate; interpretation remains limited by sample size, episode dependence assumptions and the simulator.

Apply the predeclared service floor and comparison rule. Simulator evidence can support a bounded trial proposal, hold or no-go decision. It does not establish that an actual warehouse will be safe.

No. The floor is a decision constraint, not a decorative metric. Record hold or no-go under the declared rule, name the fallback, and state the additional evidence or redesign needed.

Predeclare one public development stress case, such as a changed shortage-cost coefficient, while keeping policies frozen. Explain why that is sensitivity evidence, not a replacement for the protected batch.

7 MIN READ · AT YOUR PACESUPPORT / CORE / EXTENSION

Your final product is a defensible recommendation

The capstone brings together evidence produced during the existing six sessions. It asks whether the forecasting release and the policy release support a bounded operational decision. It does not ask you to invent another model, build a joined forecasting controller, or attend a seventh practical. A clear recommendation to hold a release can be stronger work than an unsupported recommendation to deploy.

Start the shared repository in TD1 and maintain it as each session closes. Alternate the experiment-lead and evidence-steward roles. Record contributions and AI assistance as the work happens. The final curation allowance is already declared in the capstone brief: six to eight out-of-class hours per pair. This book introduces no additional compulsory workload.

Three roles give the evidence a spine

Submit three to six claims. One required claim traces forecasting from the temporal contract and baselines through development comparison to the protected 28-day receipt. A second traces the MDP, reward and comparator policies through frozen learning to controlled evaluation. A third synthesises the operational safety decision and cites those two source claims. Up to three supporting claims may capture a specific failure, interval or sensitivity result.

Suppose, as a hypothetical release discussion, the forecast improves development MAE but has weak interval coverage, while the learned policy fails the service floor. The synthesis should not average those concerns into a general success score. State which forecast use might remain acceptable, which policy should be held, which fallback applies, and what additional evidence is needed. These are invented conditions, not course findings.

Make the handoff reproducible and readable

Every claim row should resolve to exact files at the immutable submitted tag. Include configuration and data hashes, decisive figures, controlled receipts, the contribution record and the AI-use record. The brief is at most four equivalent pages, excluding references. Tell the reader the decision first, then the evidence and limits needed to assess it.

The prescribed quick replay runs the two frozen strand commands. It checks decisive public outputs; it cannot recreate protected evaluation. It must not retrain, tune or search for favourable seeds. Preserve a failed replay and revise the affected claim status rather than manufacturing a success receipt.

Forecast origins and inventory episodes are different evaluation populations. No experiment in the course establishes that the forecast feeds the RL environment. An end-to-end efficiency or production-safety claim would therefore exceed the evidence. Use the full capstone brief as the submission authority.

Pause and decide

A controlled receipt is delayed by the instructor. Should the pair invent final numbers or be penalised for the missing handoff?

Compare your reasoning

Neither. Record the pending evidence and follow the brief’s deadline rule, which depends on both receipts being returned. A missing institutional handoff is not permission to fabricate a result.

Support · Trace one claim backwards

Read one conclusion aloud. Open its result file, configuration and source receipt in that order. If any link is missing, fix the traceability or narrow the claim before polishing the prose.

Extension · Design the next honest experiment

After the required release is complete, outline a small future study that could change the decision. Specify the unresolved question, comparator, data boundary, stopping rule and evidence you would accept against your preferred method.
